Mount Frere, Eastern Cape

A safe door to walk through, before things get worse.

Founded by Lindokuhle Kentane, Unako exists because most people in Mount Frere don't know where to go when abuse starts — long before it becomes an emergency.

Gender-based violence rarely begins with a bruise. It usually starts with control, isolation and words — and by the time it turns physical, many women feel they can no longer leave, especially where they depend on a partner financially. Unako is built around that gap: a place to talk before the emergency, not only after it.

We are working toward a multi-use youth development centre that combines a reporting and counselling space with everything else young people need to build a future — a media suite, mentoring, and workshops that give them somewhere to belong that isn't the street corner or the situation they're trying to leave.

Beyond crisis support, Unako wants to be the place that catches young people before drugs, unemployment or early pregnancy narrow their options — through mentoring, careers workshops, and a stage for the talent that rural Eastern Cape rarely gets to show off.

"It is sad to watch young people give up on their future through things they were never given the tools to avoid. We believe fixing that mindset can change a village."

There is a real need for this centre in the community: many young people give up on life because of high unemployment, teenage pregnancy remains common, and rural areas often lack the education and role models to help young people avoid drugs and alcohol. Unako's founder believes that fixing that mindset, one young person at a time, can make the villages a better place to grow up.

— Lindokuhle Kentane, Founder

Who's involved

Built with the village, not just for it.

Unako is designed to work alongside the people already showing up for Mount Frere's youth — schools, health providers, and local leadership — rather than compete with them.

Local schools Youth Council Peer supporters Health providers SAPS & social workers Local municipality Sports clubs Corporate partners

  • Manager

    Runs day-to-day operations, fundraising, and community liaison, full-time and on site.

  • Youth worker(s)

    Deliver programmes both at the centre and out in Mount Frere's outlying areas.

  • Volunteers

    Trained community members who also travel to remote areas to teach what GBV is and what to do about it.

  • Specialist roles

    Social worker, sound engineer, security guard and driver, added as funding allows.
