01 — About Unako

A safe door to walk through, before things get worse.

Founded by Lindokuhle Kentane, Unako exists because most people in Mount Frere don't know where to go when abuse starts — long before it becomes an emergency.

Gender-based violence rarely begins with a bruise. It usually starts with control, isolation and words — and by the time it turns physical, many women feel they can no longer leave, especially where they depend on a partner financially. Unako is built around that gap: a place to talk before the emergency, not only after it.

We are working toward a multi-use youth development centre that combines a reporting and counselling space with everything else young people need to build a future — a media suite, mentoring, and workshops that give them somewhere to belong that isn't the street corner or the situation they're trying to leave.

"It is sad to watch young people give up on their future through things they were never given the tools to avoid. We believe fixing that mindset can change a village."

Beyond crisis support, Unako wants to be the place that catches young people before drugs, unemployment or early pregnancy narrow their options — through mentoring, careers workshops, and a stage for the talent that rural Eastern Cape rarely gets to show off.

— Lindokuhle Kentane, Founder

02 — What we offer

Support that meets people where they are.

Every service is designed to be walked into without shame — whether the visit is about a relationship, a habit, or a job application.

GBV response and counselling

A confidential first point of contact for anyone experiencing or leaving an abusive relationship, with guidance on reporting and next steps.

Family and shelter support

Working with local government toward emergency shelter for those who need to get away from an abuser, plus support for the family left navigating the fallout.

Mental health support

Space to talk through trauma, anxiety and the everyday weight of rural life, with mentors and, over time, professional counsellors.

Drug and alcohol support

Honest, non-judgemental support for young people and adults dealing with addiction, and the family members affected by it.

Employment workshops

CV writing, interview practice and links to local opportunities, so a fresh start isn't blocked by not knowing where to begin.

Sexual and reproductive health

Clear, stigma-free information and referrals on sexual health, delivered by people trained to talk to young people honestly.

Healthy relationships

Workshops on what a respectful, safe partnership looks like — aimed at catching the warning signs long before they escalate.

03 — The Centre

What a visit to Unako looks like.

Once open, the centre runs as both a drop-in space and a booked venue — for individuals, schools, and youth groups.

i.

Arrive, no appointment needed

Reception and a dining hall welcome walk-ins alongside groups who've booked ahead — nobody has to explain why they're there.

ii.

Talk, in private

A counsellor's office and crisis room handle GBV cases, family issues and mental health conversations, away from the open floor.

iii.

Build something else

The media suite, performance space and outdoor courts run in parallel — sport, music and IT skills for whoever's booked in.

iv.

Leave with a next step

Whether that's a referral, a CV, a rehearsal recording, or just having been heard — every visit ends with somewhere to go next.

04 — Who's involved

Built with the village, not just for it.

Unako is designed to work alongside the people already showing up for Mount Frere's youth — schools, health providers, and local leadership — rather than compete with them.

Local schools Youth Council Peer supporters Health providers SAPS & social workers Local municipality Sports clubs Corporate partners

  • Manager

    Runs day-to-day operations, fundraising, and community liaison, full-time and on site.

  • Youth worker(s)

    Deliver programmes both at the centre and out in Mount Frere's outlying areas.

  • Volunteers

    Trained community members who also travel to remote areas to teach what GBV is and what to do about it.

  • Specialist roles

    Social worker, sound engineer, security guard and driver, added as funding allows.
