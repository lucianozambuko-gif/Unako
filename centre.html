The build

What a visit to Unako looks like.

Once open, the centre runs as both a drop-in space and a booked venue — for individuals, schools, and youth groups, on weekdays and weekends alike.

i.

Arrive, no appointment needed

Reception and a dining hall welcome walk-ins alongside groups who've booked ahead — nobody has to explain why they're there.

ii.

Talk, in private

A counsellor's office and crisis room handle GBV cases, family issues and mental health conversations, away from the open floor.

iii.

Build something else

The media suite, performance space and outdoor courts run in parallel — sport, music and IT skills for whoever's booked in.

iv.

Leave with a next step

Whether that's a referral, a CV, a rehearsal recording, or just having been heard — every visit ends with somewhere to go next.

Who will use it

A venue for the whole community, not one group.

Youth groups, sports and cultural groups can book organised events, while corporates can hire the space for team-building — income that flows straight back into youth programmes. Visiting speakers, tutors and career-focused organisations will run regular sessions alongside individual drop-ins.

Youth & sports groups Schools & students Corporates (team-building) Tutors & counsellors Visiting speakers Career & training bodies
What the centre still needs

Everything from land to light bulbs.

Land & building

  • Land, granted by the village chief
  • Perimeter fence and gate
  • Reception and dining hall
  • Manager and counsellor offices
  • Separate male and female toilets
  • Borehole for water

Programme & media

  • Media suite: computers, Wi-Fi, projector
  • Recording and performance space
  • Video camera
  • Outdoor area for sport
  • Tent, speakers and mics for events

Running the centre

  • Kitchen and appliances
  • Tables, chairs and storeroom
  • A centre vehicle and fuel budget
  • Security at the gate
  • Salaries for staff and counsellors
